Built from the Act I–V structure, cross-referenced against the locked Master Timeline and Mystery/Clue Ledger. Columns: **POV**, **Timestamp**, **What changes by chapter's end**, **Mystery/clue served**, **Illustration style**.

## ✅ DECIDED — Single POV, Kwame only

This is a real structural commitment, not a cosmetic one — it changes several chapters that were previously written as scenes in Nanfuri's, Mansa's, Saaba's, or Abena's heads. Rather than cut that content, it's been converted using techniques already native to your own draft (Nene narrating events to Kwame is literally already how your source material handles this) plus two more: **direct conversation** (a character tells Kwame something to his face, which can carry real emotional weight — arguably more than a cutaway scene) and **evidence/testimony** (Kwame encounters proof, not narration, and has to interpret it).

**Honest trade-off to flag:** single POV means Act IV in particular (Ch. 17–21, the investigation) can no longer be dramatized as police procedural scenes, since Kwame is in custody and not present for most of it. Those chapters are reworked below as "Kwame is informed/visited" chapters instead. This is a legitimate, common structure for wrongful-accusation thrillers (the reader is as cut off from the investigation as the protagonist is, which can heighten tension rather than dilute it) — but it does mean less on-page procedural detail than a rotating-POV version would have had. If that ends up feeling thin once you're drafting Act IV, the fix is to give Kwame more visitors/information sources (a lawyer, in particular, isn't currently in your cast and might be worth adding), not to break the single-POV rule.

**One mystery-mechanics consequence, also flagged in the Ledger:** Mystery 1's "dramatic irony" device (the reader seeing Mansa's fall before Kwame does) required a Mansa POV scene and no longer works as originally locked. Revised below and in the Ledger — the irony now comes from Kwame noticing clues that don't fit the town's story, not from the reader having privileged access Kwame lacks.

## Net effect of the single-POV decision

- **Chapter count effectively drops from 27 to ~25** — Ch. 10 and 11 no longer stand alone; their content is absorbed into Ch. 14
- **Act IV is now an "accused man learns what's happening to him" act rather than a procedural** — intentional and often more tense, but worth deliberately enriching with visitor scenes so it doesn't read as static
- **Ch. 9 and Ch. 21 are the two chapters that most need craft attention when drafted** — both are carrying content that used to live in a dedicated POV chapter, now compressed into someone else's account. Give both real scene-weight (setting, emotional stakes, not just information transfer) rather than letting them read as summary

## Still open (unaffected by this decision)

- Saaba's want/need — now matters slightly differently, since it needs to land through what Kwame gathers about her rather than her own interiority, but is still undefined
- Abena's pre-argument involvement — Ch. 23 now assumes this is delivered as her own confession to Kwame if adopted; still not confirmed as canon
