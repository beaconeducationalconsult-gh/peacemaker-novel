# Mallam Town — Illustration Style Guide

Two deliberate visual modes, assigned by scene tone rather than by chapter number. The split itself does thematic work: Style A (danger/night) vs. Style B (peace/day) mirrors the book's own "peace is not the same as justice" throughline.

---

## STYLE A — Scratchboard Noir
**Use for:** night scenes, danger, pursuit, crime, the attack, the manhunt, anything where the community's calm has broken.

**Core prompt block (reuse verbatim, then add scene specifics):**
> black and white scratchboard-style ink illustration, high-contrast, white linework carved out of a near-solid black background, single dramatic light source (moonlight, flashlight beam, headlights) rendered as a pale wedge or cone cutting through the darkness, dense fine-line cross-hatching used for texture on foliage/brick/fabric, sparse or absent detail in flat black areas (sky, unlit ground) to direct the eye, graphic-novel noir aesthetic, value achieved through black/white contrast only — no greyscale shading, cinematic voyeuristic or tense framing

**Checklist per scene:**
- [ ] Identify the single light source and describe it as a wedge/beam, not ambient glow
- [ ] Name what stays in near-total black (this is where mood lives)
- [ ] If there's a concealed/watching figure, frame through foreground foliage or an obstruction (per Image 1)
- [ ] Keep line density uneven — dense on hair/foliage/texture, sparse on open ground/sky

---

## STYLE B — Clean Line Daylight
**Use for:** daytime, domestic, procedural-but-safe scenes — the station in normal operation, home before the argument, Nene's house, interiors, anything establishing the "peaceful" baseline.

**Core prompt block (reuse verbatim, then add scene specifics):**
> clean black-outline line illustration on white background, uniform line weight throughout, minimal sparse hatching used only for shading, no solid black fills, flat even daylight with no directional shadow modeling, storybook/coloring-book illustration style, careful economical linework on everyday objects and fabric texture, quiet observational framing, figures often shown from behind or in profile, unhurried composition

**Checklist per scene:**
- [ ] Confirm it's flat daylight — no beam/glow light source language (that's Style A's job)
- [ ] Keep shading to a few short parallel hatch marks max, never a filled black area
- [ ] Let ordinary objects (pots, stools, baskets, bowls) get the same outline care as people — that's what makes it feel lived-in rather than sparse
