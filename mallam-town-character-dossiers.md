# Mallam Town — Character Dossiers

One page per major character. The "What they know & when" section is your spoiler-control tool — check it before writing any scene from that character's POV, so they never reveal (or react to) something they haven't learned yet in the timeline.

---

## Kwame Asante — "Peacemaker"

- **Age/role:** 44, taxi driver, station master, elder at his Church of Christ congregation
- **Want:** Peace — specifically, to avoid confrontation and let noise die down on its own
- **Need:** To learn that silence is not the same as peace, and that some truths have to be spoken even at cost
- **Secret:** None sinister — he's genuinely innocent of both charges. His closest thing to a secret is mundane and human: he's in debt from covering the egg-seller's loss and hasn't told Mansa
- **Contradiction:** The same immovability that makes him beloved (never escalates, never explains himself) is exactly what reads as guilt to the town, and what nearly gets him killed when he refuses the robbers even at gunpoint
- **What he knows & when:** Knows nothing about Mansa's fall until Nene tells him (Day 1 evening) what the town believes — not what's actually true. Doesn't learn the truth about Saaba's death or the land-guard connection until Act V. Never personally witnesses the recording's discovery.
- **Voice:** Calm, terse, doesn't explain himself. Checks the preacher's Bible verses for accuracy rather than arguing. Silence is his primary form of communication — dialogue should often be what he *doesn't* say.
- **Arc:** Silence-as-peace → forced confrontation with what silence costs → learns to speak

## Mansa

- **Age/role:** 38, Kwame's wife, longest-serving tenant alongside him
- **Want:** Security and reassurance — proof that Kwame isn't hiding something from her (money, another woman)
- **Need:** To trust rather than surveil; her suspicion is a symptom of fear, not malice
- **Secret:** She's the one who caused her own fall — she tried to follow Kwame out through the window and fell. Not pushed. She knows this the whole time she's unconscious-to-conscious; the reader/other characters don't.
- **Contradiction:** Her sharpest accusation ("you're good for nothing") is really a fear of abandonment turned into an attack
- **What she knows & when:** Knows the true sequence of her own fall throughout — there's no discovery moment for her, only for everyone else once she wakes and confesses. Doesn't know what happened to Kwame or Saaba until she's told post-recovery.
- **Voice:** Sharp, direct, emotionally exposed — the opposite of Kwame's restraint. Her dialogue should escalate where his goes quiet.
- **Arc:** Jealousy/suspicion (now canonically stoked by Abena's advice) → the fall as consequence → confession/accountability upon waking
- **✅ Decided — instigation:** Her decision to follow Kwame through the window was based on advice given by Abena (see Abena dossier). This locks Abena's role as active instigator, not passive listener.

## Abena

- **Role:** Tenant, Mansa's confidante, "Nkonkonsa" (known for gossip)
- **Want:** Social currency — something to bring back to her circle of friends at the salon
- **Need:** To reckon with the real cost of what she treats as entertainment
- **✅ Decided — active instigation (canon):** Abena *did* actively encourage Mansa's suspicion before the argument. The recommended beat is now locked: a line like "you still think I should follow him?" delivered before 11:42 p.m., which Mansa then acts on when she attempts to sneak out the window after Kwame leaves. This makes her arc sharper — instigator → reckoner, not just witness → reckoner — and gives Ch. 4 and Ch. 23 their dramatic purpose.
- **Contradiction:** Enjoys gossip as entertainment right up until it's someone else's ruin
- **What she knows & when:** Overhears the full 11:42 p.m. argument through the wall. Present for the CID's first questioning at the house (Day 1 morning). Carries private guilt from having encouraged Mansa from the start — a guilt she holds the whole book, which the reader may or may not be shown early. This private guilt is now the core of her interiority.
- **Voice:** Nervous and deferential under authority (the CID officers), chatty and confident otherwise
- **Arc:** Instigator → guilt → the one who must confess her role late in the book (Ch. 23). Her confession to Kwame is now a required plot beat.

## Chief Inspector Nanfuri

- **Age/role:** 57, police commander, 30 years in service, Saaba's father
- **Want:** Catch the masked man; restore order; professionally, closure on a clean case — and, once Saaba dies, to find who killed his daughter
- **Need:** To reconcile procedure and restraint with grief — and to forgive himself for the choice he made at the cemetery on Day 0
- **Secret:** He senses something is deeply wrong at the cemetery on Day 0 and makes the disciplined choice not to pursue alone — then has to live with that choice once it's his own daughter who dies nearby, not knowing at the time that the two are connected. **Additionally, he is the person Saaba was on the phone with when the robbers entered** — he hears the attack begin on the call. The house recording of that same call later provides the clearer background voice/name that becomes actionable evidence.
- **Contradiction:** A famously careful, methodical officer whose one moment of restraint becomes the thing he has to justify to himself for the rest of the book — now compounded by having been on the phone as his daughter was attacked
- **What he knows & when:** Witnesses the full Day 0 chase and cemetery scene. Is on the phone with Saaba when the robbery/murder begins on Night of Day 0 — hears the intrusion start, then the call cuts. Learns of Saaba's death Day 1 morning. Eventually the house recording (of that same call) surfaces, is clarified, and is brought to Chocho for outside-group identification; Nanfuri leads the final arrest back at the cemetery.
- **Voice:** Clipped commands under pressure, murmurs to himself when alone and turning something over — his interiority is where the reader gets the cemetery's dread in Ch. 3 (now delivered to Kwame secondhand via Ch. 21 conversation)
- **Arc:** Guarded professional restraint → personal reckoning with his daughter's death *while having heard it begin* → justice tempered by what he saw and chose not to act on

## Saaba

- **Role:** Nanfuri's daughter, the murder victim
- **✅ Decided — call identity (canon):** She was on the phone with her father, Chief Inspector Nanfuri, when the robbers entered. The recording found at her house is of this father-daughter call — it captures her reaction and a background voice that lets slip a name. This gives the recording its full emotional and investigative weight: the victim's last call was to the officer who will have to investigate it, and Nanfuri himself was on the other end when it began.
- **Want:** To build an independent adult life in her new house while staying close to her father — the call itself is the expression of that want (checking in, ordinary connection). This keeps her human and ordinary rather than secretly entangled.
- **Need:** No hidden flaw required — her power as a character comes from normalcy interrupted, not from a secret. She does not need a redemption arc; she needs to feel like a real daughter on an ordinary night.
- **Secret:** None — she has no knowledge of the land-guard network or Chocho; she is targeted opportunistically for the house (newest, unregistered, assumed rich owner), not personally. This remains true.
- **What she knows & when:** She has no knowledge of the land-guard network. On the night of Day 0 she knows only that she is on a normal call with her father until the intrusion begins.
- **Voice:** Warm, direct, familiar/daughterly with her father — should read as an ordinary young woman on an ordinary call (so the reader feels the interruption, not a thriller-heroine performance). Keep her dialogue natural and brief, since it will be heard only via the recording and via Nanfuri's secondhand account to Kwame.
- **Arc:** Ordinary life → victim — even a victim who dies in Ch. 9 benefits from this one clear, human note (independent but close to father) that the reader carries forward into the recording in Ch. 18-19.

## Nene

- **Role:** Herbalist, elder, protector — the one person who doubts the town's conspiracy theories from the start
- **Want:** To protect Kwame and do right by what she believes is true
- **Need:** ✅ **Decided — fixed anchor.** Nene functions best as a stable moral anchor rather than someone with her own flaw to resolve. She does not need a separate internal arc; she is the fixed point other characters move around. Her steadiness is the point.
- **Secret:** None currently established
- **Contradiction:** Gentle healer, but firm enough to stand off an armed search team alone
- **What she knows & when:** Learns everything Kwame can tell her about the attack (Day 1 evening) — which is incomplete, since he doesn't know about Mansa's fall or Saaba's death beyond what Nene herself tells him
- **Voice:** Practical and unsentimental with the search team, gentle and patient with Kwame
- **Arc:** ✅ **Decided — fixed anchor, no transformation arc.** Passive doubter → active protector is her only movement, and it is action, not interior change. She remains constant; the change happens around her. Do not give her a late investigator turn — that would dilute her function.

## Chocho

- **Role:** Leader of the land guards in Mallam Town
- **Want:** Maintain his network's local standing and power; avoid his own crew being implicated in a high-profile murder
- **Need:** Functions primarily as a plot-service/grey-zone character — his need beyond self-preservation can remain lightly sketched. A grey-zone antagonist who's genuinely not guilty of *this* crime but is complicit in a coercive local economy is more interesting if he has some personal stake beyond self-preservation, but this is optional texture, not a required arc.
- **Secret:** Knows the land-guard networks across neighboring communities well enough to identify a rival crew's voice on the recording — implying he has more visibility into that world than he lets on to police
- **Contradiction:** Helps solve the case (genuinely, not performatively) while representing exactly the kind of extortion economy the case exposes — the book doesn't need to resolve this tension, but shouldn't pretend it isn't there
- **What he knows & when:** Enlisted by police for the Day 1 manhunt (his own motives for cooperating are worth a line of interiority — self-interest and genuine civic instinct aren't mutually exclusive). Later identifies the recording's voice as belonging to an outside group.
- **Voice:** Guarded, transactional, commands quiet fear/respect locally
- **Arc:** Suspect-by-association (per community gossip and the reader's red-herring suspicion) → cooperator, though not necessarily redeemed — his cooperation can be self-interested and still be true
