# Chapter 17 — Two Charges

*Day 2 onward — custody, interrogation*

The room where they laid the case had flat light.

Style B, clean daylight, no beam. White walls, black outline uniform, minimal hatching on the table's edge and on the pen the officer held, no solid fills, no directional shadows. A storybook room for a story that was not a story but a filing.

Kwame sat where he had been put the previous day, after the rope became handcuffs. His lip had been cleaned. His eye opened more. His ribs still made breathing a counted thing. Someone had given him water in a sachet, not a bottle, and he had drunk it because Nene was not there to hold it.

The officer across the table was not the one who had written the first statement. This one was older, not Nanfuri — Nanfuri, whose daughter's name was now the other charge, would not be in this room yet — but a CID man from town who used the word *fact* the way preachers used *holy*, to start sentences.

“Fact,” he said, tapping the first paper, “argument for night. Whole compound hear. ‘Good for nothing’ — neighbour hear. You leave. Gate no screech — you lift am, we hear you lift am every morning. You leave 11:42.”

Kwame did not answer. He was listening for where the facts would turn into a story.

“Fact two,” the officer said, turning a page so Kwame could see the drawing — not a drawing, a photo taken through the window that he had not looked beyond. White curtain, sill, pawpaw leaves big as hands, ground where a woman had lain. “Window open, curtain blow, Mansa for ground. Phone off. You search kitchen, not outside window. Why you no check window, Mr. Asante?”

The answer — *I think say she walk through door like people who want compound to hear* — sat in his mouth and did not come out in three sentences. He said, “I call phone — dead.”

“Dead,” the officer repeated, not doubting the word but the meaning. He wrote *claims phone dead from fall* but said aloud, “Or switched off because she vex?” Which was exactly the red herring the Ledger had planned: unreachable reads as deliberate.

Second charge.

The officer slid a second file, thinner, darker.

“Fact,” he said again. “Your car — Toyota Corolla, fourteen years — find for that seven-house house. New house, glass windows, fine girl. Saaba. Chief Inspector Nanfuri pikin. We find am dead for floor, phone record dey.”

He did not play the recording yet. That belonged to the next chapter, to Saaba's House. He let the car be the villain.

“Witness see your car break barrier that afternoon? No — Camry break barrier. But your car reach there night same road. People know say new house, fresh paper no dey — land guard see. So motive say you fit target rich police pikin. Or you owe money — ₵300, we hear — so you need money, you rob. Two stories, same car.”

Kwame's hands, cuffed loosely to the table ring, made a small sound against metal — not trying to break, just trying to be still.

“Which be true?” the officer asked, as if giving him a choice between two wrong things.

Kwame thought of that morning at six — the Madam, the thirty eggs, Kofi's elbow, ₵150 and ₵150 borrowed, the way he had said *I go pay* to Ebo. He thought of Last Stop at midnight, the three people at the gravel edge: *You go Lapaz or no?* Extra ₵50, pistol low along the door, hand reaching past his chest to kill ignition, wire snapping, gravel on cheek where potholes found you. He thought of the uncompleted building, distinct from the cemetery's cleared paths and stash point, and of Nene's hand on his wrist.

He wanted to say *car steal* — two words that should be enough — and then add the many words that would make two words believable. He knew, from the station, that two words without the many words reads as excuse. So he said the least that was not silence, which had already proved dangerous:

“They steal car for Last Stop,” he said. Voice terse, not explaining beyond what needed to be known, even now. “I refuse. They thief am.”

The officer wrote: *claims carjacking at Last Stop/junction.*

“Who be ‘they’?”

That was the question the recording would answer — background voice, name slipped, Chocho saying *outside group, not Mallam* — but that recording was still seven houses away in a house where a phone had been left on speaker and a father had heard a line cut. Not here. Not yet.

Kwame did not know their names. He knew their shape: tall, woman with bag, leader who photographed before shooting. He knew their method: ask, offer, threaten, reach.

“Three,” he said. “Two man, one woman.”

The officer nodded without surprise. Three had been said before by other drivers about other robberies. Three was not yet a clue.

“Fact three,” the officer said, tapping both files together, making one case. “You missing overnight, till 4:15 next day for uncompleted building. Town search, Chocho help. You hide for Nene house. Why hide if no do?”

Why hide. Because bleeding, because the town had already decided *you push*, because Nene had said *stay* and meant *stay*. Because missing reads as flight when quiet reads as guilt.

Kwame looked at the flat light, the uniform outline, the careful economical linework on the water sachet, and said what he had said the previous day, because it was still the only whole true thing he had that was not a story about eggs or a story about a wall:

“I no do am. Both.”

The officer wrote. The flat light stayed flat. The case, built entirely from what he *didn't* say or do, not what he did — the institutional version of the theme — sat between them, thin but tidy, like a queue that had formed correctly behind the wrong destination sign.

Outside, a transistor that someone had left on for the charge room, not for him, gave Kasoa FM's midday voice reading a funeral and then, after a small pause, a new, shorter item read with that same emotion that made text sound bigger than it was: *recording found at seven-house house…*

The officer did not turn it up. He noted it. Later, in Ch.18, Kwame would be told properly what had been found at Saaba's house — the call she was on with her father when robbers entered — but here, in Two Charges, it was enough that the town already knew something had been found and therefore the case, in its two-charge form, felt consolidated.

The officer stood. Handcuffs stayed but the ring on the table was released.

“You rest,” he said, not unkind. “We go bring your visitor tomorrow.”

Visitor. Singular. Nene had been first. The next — per the reworked outline, to keep Act IV from being static — would need to be someone who could carry the recording into this daylight room. For now, the room stayed flat, bright, and storybook — safe, despite its content — while the man who had chosen silence at 11:42 sat with the cost of that silence filed in duplicate.
