# Chapter 20 — The Missing Minutes

*Later — same period, custody — flat light*

The case was not wrong in facts. It was wrong in minutes.

That thought came to Kwame not as a lawyer's thought — he was not a lawyer — but as a driver's. He sat with the flat light — uniform outline, minimal hatching, white walls, storybook linework on the table's empty surface — and counted the way he counted sales or the way he counted how long a Kasoa run should take on a road where potholes were visible by day and found you at night.

He counted because the station had trained him to know how long a run should take, how many minutes a queue should need to fill, how much dust should settle between one departure and the next. Cases, he was learning, are also routes.

Ebo's visit had said *recording found at seven-house house — girl dey phone father — background Musah, speaker on counter, not clean*. The interrogator with *fact* had said *argument 11:42, you leave, gate no screech because you lift am every morning, Mansa for pawpaw tree base, phone dead, curtain draft, neighbour hear good for nothing*. The constable with the phone copy had played *Daddy? You dey house? I lock —* and *Make Musah bring car comot — fast — no make headlight catch wall —* and *Saaba! Saaba!* before cut and the two seconds of house dust after.

He laid them as a driver lays a route on a map that is also a town's gossip path.

11:42 — he leaves, walks to Last Stop/junction — same barrier that a tinted, no-plate Toyota Camry broke at 4 p.m. with a hollow thwack and sent its striped pole swinging, same junction where he had counted ₵285 that afternoon and where the young policeman had straightened the pole afterwards. He sits at the low wall, still warm, elbows on knees, trying silence as peacemaking. Calls Mansa — dead, not switched off, flat nothing like a cut line, like the cut he would later hear on Saaba's recording. Receives Yaw's call about stranded passengers at 12:03 — *Station master, two passengers stranded for Last Stop side* — he stands, walks back the lane, lifts gate, quiet, finds empty room not like sleeping but flat dark of no one, sales pouch on car not table, kitchen plates washed, towel damp, window open a hand's breadth, curtain moving with cool draft on ankle, does not look down where pawpaw leaves are big as hands, takes car keys from nail, leaves, drives headlight wedge back to Last Stop. Short. Eight minutes, perhaps ten, if you do not hurry.

12:25 — flagged at gravel edge where tar thins, where the road chooses cemetery one way and uncompleted bush the other, by three — two men and a woman with small bag, same three Nanfuri chased that afternoon, same who photographed before shooting — *You go Lapaz or no?* Refuses, plain. Extra ₵50 — refuses, even with that making ₵345 > ₵300. Pistol low along door, angled up — refuses, even then, terse, because he is looking for wife not fare. Hand reaching past chest to kill ignition, across Bible-at-Matthew-5 chest, wire snapping where he had fixed handle at dawn, door wrenched, dragged, gravel on cheek at the exact spot where he had earlier counted money and where barrier had been hit, beating — not efficient, woman saying *make we take car leave am!*, tall man watching toward station where stranded passengers were not, leader doing most — dragged off road, through bush that scratches, into uncompleted building, distinct from cemetery's cleared path/stash point/crooked iron gate, half-wall, sand, sachets, rebar, phone cracked on chest where tall man placed it, Corolla starts second try and goes not to Lapaz but seven houses along same main road to Saaba's new house where lights are still warm and a kitchen window's low side is three steps from a counter where a phone is on speaker.

Inside that seven-house house, ordinary call already open on speaker before car even stops: *Daddy, you lock?* *You push that kitchen window well?* Musah at window.

That was one story — the true one, which he had lived bodily — gravel, wire, tooth moving — and not been believed because the town's second nervous system had already filed a shorter, smoother story that, like shorter routes, skipped potholes.

The filed story was shorter and smoother and therefore more believed:

*You argue at 11:42, you push Mansa for window because she calls you good for nothing or because you hide money/woman, you switch off phone deliberately, you drive your own Corolla seven houses to kill police pikin for that fresh house for revenge or money or to target Nanfuri, you run to Nene hut, we find you behind door, rope, tie, jubilation.*

Shorter stories are often smoother than true ones, because true ones have missing minutes where a man sits at a wall and being true makes you late.

Kwame counted those missing minutes aloud, not to the officer but to the table's linework, where ordinary objects would have been if ordinary objects were needed.

If he had pushed Mansa at 11:42, why would he walk to Last Stop and sit with his back to the police post and not lean? Pushers do not sit at junctions where police posts are, where barriers have been hit and photographed, where a commander will later say *Carefully*. Pushers do not call a dead phone four times to hear dead not off. Pushers hide cars, do not take borrowed-money cars that still hold ₵295 + ₵20 parish money to rob glass-window houses with lights on where a father is on speaker and where the house's freshness is exactly why land guards would see it — *new house, no wall finish — na invitation*.

If he had driven to Saaba's house to kill because his car was his motive, why would he first go home for the car *after* finding Mansa not in the room — a fact he would later be told was because she was already outside under the pawpaw tree after acting on Abena's advice — and why would the robbers need to ask him *You go Lapaz or no?* twice, offer extra, draw pistol, reach in to kill ignition — mechanics that matched the Camry crew's afternoon method (photograph before shooting, then tyre) — if the car were lent? Lent cars do not need door-wrenching.

If Musah was his boy — his land-guard tie — why would Chocho — whose Lacoste was flat, whose slippers were quiet, whose men had helped search and whose suggestion *make boys check Nene side last* had been heard at 4:40 and read as suspicious — be brought to hear Musah on the copy and say *not my boy — outside group, different community, Musah be their driver — fresh house work — not Mallam*, and why would that identifier also explain the Camry's barrier break as outside work on fresh-paper economics?

And the window: a cool draft moving a white curtain he himself had hem-watched Mansa make. He had felt it on his ankle at 12:18 at the sill, low, worn where hands grip, and tugged it inward irritably, as if draft were the problem. A constable at mid-morning, with a draft from outside not inside, had seen the same curtain move and looked, and through it seen what Kwame had not — Mansa at pawpaw tree base, faint pulse, rope barricade. Between those two drafts — his 12:18 and the constable's ~9:30next day — a woman lay with a phone dead from impact, not choice, and a bang that someone in the compound heard and, not knowing where it came from, ignored — the plantable clue that, if heard properly, would have changed everything, the way Nene had heard a different sound — slippers at a doorway, not a bang — and not ignored but put her body between doorway and half-wall.

The missing minutes were not missing from his story. They were missing from the filed story, which had made flight from *empty room* and *dead phone* without accounting for *seven houses* distance that the stolen car needed to be driven, or *Musah* that needed an outside identifier, or *window open before search* that needed a draft not a push, or *uncompleted building* that was not cemetery but was notorious and therefore first place to look and place you look quickly.

He did not shout this reasoning. Shouting was for egg mobs, for arrests, for *Stoooooppppp!!* — reserved, the guide said, for high intensity so this low intensity could be more true. He wrote it, small, in the margin of the form they had given him to sign — not as a lawyer, but as a station master who kept queues tight and who knew that a queue behind the wrong destination sign looks correct until you read the sign:

*Last Stop = barrier (same), 7 houses to Saaba house, car steal not lent (reach-in kill + wire snap), phone dead not off (impact), window open + draft before search (pawpaw), outside group (Chocho: not my boy, Musah outside), uncompleted ≠ cemetery (bush vs cleared stash).*

Small. Legible. Terse as his checking of verses.

The officer who collected the form glanced at the margin, did not argue — officers in flat rooms rarely argue margins; they argue facts — just folded it.

“We go bring Commander tomorrow morning,” he said, not *if you want*. “He go tell you what he see for cemetery gate for dusk. That one no dey file. You go hear with mouth, not paper.”

Kwame nodded. Paper had given him *fact*. Mouth would give him dread that was rational and grief that was human.

The flat room kept its storybook linework, careful on ordinary objects — sachet, pen, stamp felt — while the man who had kept quiet long enough for noise to die down now, from custody, started to notice that the case against him did not add up on its own logic — investigative payoff, but from the accused's side, where noticing matters more than access and where missing minutes are the only minutes that prove the route was not the one filed.

Outside, Kasoa FM turned down for night after a small, unthrilling *protect your documents* without added emotions. The hill's gate — which he had muffled every night with a palm lift, no screech, to keep peace — waited for the chapter where he would, once, let it screech on purpose, small, to show change that was not noise but choice.
