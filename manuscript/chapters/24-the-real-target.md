# Chapter 24 — The Real Target

*Same period — at his own hearing, custody*

They told him at the hearing, not in the cell.

The room was still flat light, but more people made it a hearing. A table longer, a man with a stamp, a constable at the door who did not offer tea. Kwame stood because they told him to stand, not because he wanted to. His cuffed hands were in front, not behind — a small, procedural mercy for a man whose file now had two charges and two confessions that cleared only one.

One charge had begun to clear: *Mansa — accident — window — on Abena advice — fall, not push — phone dead by impact.* That file had a doctor's note about angle and a woman's *I fall* and a neighbor's *I told her to follow* — all secondhand to the court but first-hand to Kwame via Nene and via the curtain that had moved.

The other charge needed a different voice.

The officer with *fact* did not say *fact* in this room. He said, “We bring identifier.”

He brought Chocho.

Chocho did not look like an identifier. He looked as he had at midday in Chapter 2 — Lacoste flat, trousers dark, slippers that made no sound in dust, hair cut low and exact — but now the room gave him a chair that was not offered to show respect but to keep his slippers from being heard when he shifted. Two boys were not with him. He came alone, which in Mallam Town was a stronger statement.

He did not look at Kwame first. He looked at the room — pots and stools not there, given the same outline care as people — and then at the constable's phone, which already held the copy of the copy of the house recording: *Daddy? You dey house? I lock —* *Musah bring car —*

“Play,” the man with the stamp said.

The constable played.

Kwame had heard it twice — once on the constable's phone copy, once in his memory where wood on wood kept returning — but hearing it in a hearing with Chocho in the room was different. The background *Musah* was still low, practical, as Chocho used names, but now it had a second ear that knew what a name's network sounded like.

The recording stopped after *Saaba! Saaba!* and cut, as before.

The man with the stamp looked at Chocho.

“You know voice?”

Chocho's answer was guarded, transactional, as the dossier had promised — quiet fear/respect in the room not because he raised his voice but because everyone knew what his work was and was not.

“I know voice,” Chocho said. Not loud. Transactional in the exact degree that made it believed: self-interest and civic instinct not mutually exclusive.

He turned his palm slightly — a small, clarifying gesture that drivers use when correcting a wrong destination queue.

“Not my boy.” The words the ledger had waited for. “Musah be outside. Different community. That side boys dey do fresh house work — not paper — they see seven-house house, they use tinted Camry for work, they also do that barrier that afternoon. Not Mallam. Outside.”

The room made a small sound — not gasp, more like the way a queue does when the right sign is finally lifted.

“Why you sure?” the man with the stamp asked, because he had to.

Chocho gave the secret the dossier had noted he held and rarely let police see: he knew land-guard networks across neighboring communities well enough to identify a rival crew's voice — implying more visibility than he let on.

“Musah voice,” Chocho said, flat. “He drive for that side. He talk so — *make Musah bring car comot* — na that side slang. My boys no talk so for work. They talk *move car.* Outside boys talk *bring car comot.* I hear for land meeting before.” He shrugged, precise. “I no be friend. I be work man like you — but that house no be my target.”

That was the twist: clears his crew and gives police a concrete external lead. Helps solve case genuinely, not performatively, while representing exactly the coercive economy the case exposed — the book did not need to resolve that tension, just not to pretend it wasn't there.

The man with the stamp nodded once. He stamped something. The sound was ordinary — wood on paper — not the gate's screech nor the pawpaw thud.

Kwame stood with his hands cuffed and understood, without being told, that *Musah bring car comot* — which had lived as a background name — was now a road.

The officer leaned to the constable at the door and said, quiet, “We go cemetery. That gate.”

Kwame heard *cemetery* — east of his hill house, crooked post, cleared path, stash point, tyre track ending inside where Nanfuri had stood and said *Carefully* at dusk and withdrawn alone — and heard, in the same sentence, what *gate* now meant: not supernatural, not juju, the outside crew's operational staging ground/hideout, the place the Day 0 chase had ended and where, with backup, an arrest would close the loop.

He did not ask to go. Per the reworked outline, Kwame learns of the arrest from Nanfuri personally in Chapter 25, not by witnessing. But here, at his own hearing, he was shown the hinge that would lead there: an identifier saying *not my man*.

Chocho stood to leave. He looked at Kwame for the first time, directly.

“You refuse them for Last Stop,” he said, not as accusation but as identification — the same way he had identified Musah's cadence. “You no gree carry even at gun. That be why they thief car quick to that seven-house. Your refusal be —” He searched for the word a land-guard leader would use for a station master who took an elbow for a boy. “— straight.”

Kwame, who had refused at gunpoint because he was looking for his wife, whose immovability that morning had been admirable and that night dangerous, received that *straight* as a verdict heavier than *Bad Luck Nkoaa.*

Chocho left with slippers quiet. The hearing, which had needed a name and had gotten one, adjourned to let the road be used.

The man with the stamp turned to Kwame and, for the first time in Act IV, said a sentence that was not a charge:

“We go check. You rest.”

Rest, again. Not release. But the second of the two charges — Saaba — had, in that room, found its real target: not a jealous husband's car lent for murder, not a debt-ridden taxi master's revenge on a policeman's fine girl, but an outside crew's opportunistic work on the freshest, unpapered house, mistaken for a rich man's house, seven houses from a junction that a commander had once withdrawn from with one word.

Outside, the flat daylight held. Inside, the background voice *Musah* was now an address outside Mallam.
