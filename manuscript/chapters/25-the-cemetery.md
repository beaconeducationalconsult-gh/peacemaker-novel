# Chapter 25 — The Cemetery

*Arrest day — Kwame learns, flat room*

Nanfuri came the second time without hat as well — but this time with dust on his trousers that was not station dust, not road dust, not the yellow dust of Last Stop where barriers are hit.

It was cemetery dust — the particular grey that lives on neglected iron and on low brick walls that have been left to hold a stash.

Kwame was in the flat room again — daylight, uniform outline, minimal hatching, white ground, storybook linework on the table's edge and the constable's pen — when the door opened and the constable did not say *visitor* but *Commander*, which in Mallam Town, per the Style Guide's deference, is what subordinates call Nanfuri when they want to say *the man who stood at a gate and said Carefully* without saying *Chief Inspector*.

Nanfuri entered with the particular tiredness of a man who has done the same road twice and the second time with enough men to make the first time's careful withdrawal look like wisdom rather than hesitation. The same tiredness Kwame had seen that afternoon at six p.m. when the pickup limped back on rims, both left tyres ruined not punctured, dust to the shoulders, hat in hands, looking through the station at a gate east of a hill house.

He sat. He did not ask Kwame to sit — Kwame was already sitting where he had learned, since 17 Two Charges, that a case built from what you didn't say sits differently when someone finally says the thing that was not filed.

“We do am,” Nanfuri said, clipped, the way he gave commands under pressure — *We do am* meaning *we effect arrest*, meaning *we no lose*. Then, not clipped, the way he murmured to himself when alone turning something over, the interior voice the dossier said was where the cemetery's dread lived until it could be told directly in Ch.21 and again here: “Cemetery. Same gate. Crooked post.”

Kwame breathed. Not loud. He had breathed loud only on a floor behind a half-wall.

“Tell,” he said. Terse. But this time terse meant *I need the whole, not the fact*, terse meant *speak as you did at Last Stop's wall for me*.

Nanfuri murmured a second to himself — *hmm, hmm* — organizing not chronology but bearing, then gave it the weight the Illustration Style Guide said this chapter deserved — Style A's thematic home base, full dread in telling, fully rational in content: black and white scratchboard-style ink, high-contrast, white linework carved out of near-solid black, single dramatic light source as a pale wedge or cone cutting through darkness, dense fine-line cross-hatching on foliage/brick/fabric, sparse or absent detail in flat black sky to direct the eye, noir voyeur framing through foreground obstruction.

Though the room was daylight — B — the memory he described was all noir — A — and the book, for the first time, let both live in one scene without needing to choose.

“Daylight,” Nanfuri said. Deliberate, with the small satisfaction of a man who has corrected a mistake by doing the same thing in opposite light. “We go daylight, not night. That change. You tell your boys — potholes, you see them by day and swerve, at night they find you. That afternoon — tar to gravel to parked earth to no light — they find me with tyres. Both left. Photograph first. Now we see.”

He described it as a bearing, not a battle — methodical, clipped where clipped helps, murmured where murmuring holds dread.

“Backup — two pickups, six men, not the two constables and a flat pickup on rims that limped at six p.m. on Day 0. Not alone. Commander plus five plus driver plus radio. Chocho not there — outside group, outside road, outside need — but his *Musah is outside — different community — fresh house work — not Mallam* had drawn the line on the map that led east of your hill house, eight rooms, pawpaw tree, to the crooked post where tyre track — same tyre, same Camry, tinted no plate — ended inside on Day 0 and where, at dusk, I stepped in small, saw cleared path where no path should be cleared, small tarp, empty sachet, fresh tyre mark continuing past where eye think it stop, same as before but then you see because sun is beam, not moon.”

That beam — pale wedge — Style A's single light source, now the sun at 9 a.m. rather than moon at 9 p.m. or flashlight or headlights, cutting the cemetery's flat black of sky and unlit ground and making texture visible where flat black had hidden: cross-hatching dense on iron rust where the bar excoriates, on brick of low wall that is not a grave wall but a hideout wall, on fabric of a shirt hung to dry that should not be drying in a cemetery because no one lives in a cemetery unless they use a cemetery as a place that is not a cemetery.

Same stillness. Different honesty.

“Same stillness,” Nanfuri said, finding Kwame's eyes to make sure the word landed. “Not empty. That stillness be correct — same as before. But daylight make stillness honest: you see cleared path where head height branch cut — fresh cut, no saw dust — you see stash point — small tarp blue, empty sachet for water, fresh tyre mark for Camry continuing past where eye think it stop because you think tyre stop for gate but tyre go. Same as I see at dusk when I say *Carefully* and withdraw rather than go further alone against armed men who just shoot tyres. That *Carefully* — I say to boys, to myself — now be *With men*.”

Kwame thought of his own *carefully* — lift, push, gate no screech — every night at his compound, and of Nanfuri's *Carefully* at dusk to his boys, and how both careful had looked like fear to a town that prefers noise.

“Three inside,” Nanfuri went on. He counted the way a commander counts before a bearing. “Tall man — driver — thick man — Musah — driver as name, leader as hand — and woman with bag. Same three who stop you for Last Stop at 12:25 for *You go Lapaz or no?* Same who take your Corolla — fourteen years, wire fix Monday dawn now snapped — seven houses to Saaba house, glass window. They no run when we reach. They think gate still hide because I withdraw before at dusk, they think careful be fear, not tactic.”

He made a small, contained gesture with his hand — not a sweep, a wedge: light, then men moving along it, five plus driver, through foliage, not road — voyeur, tense — the guide's checklist: single light source as wedge, name what stays in near-total black, frame through foreground foliage, line density uneven — dense on foliage.

“We spread through foliage — frame through leaves, not road, bush — voyeur, tense, not honour road. They hear branch — not foot, branch — they look — one try back path — cleared path where head height cut — but path dey block where we put two men at 6 a.m. with hat off so no shine. No shoot. We talk first — police talk before gun — ‘Police! Come, hands.’ Musah talk first — he talk as leader talk — *we no do Saaba house, we do —* then he see my face — photographed face — before tyre — and stop. Woman say nothing — bag drop, not run. Tall man put hands first — he be driver, driver surrender first.”

*My face.* The face that had been leaned-out photographed through a tinted window before both left tyres were shot from a moving car at forty minutes' chase, tar to gravel to packed earth. The face whose daughter's small ordinary call — *Daddy, you lock?* — had been *Saaba? Saaba!* before cut and then two seconds of house dust after.

“They down — hand for back, knee for ground, not face for ground — we cuff, not rope — cuff honest. Car — your Corolla — dey under small blue tarp near stash, dust same as station dust but also cemetery dust, grey, not yellow. We bring — we also bring that Camry — same, tinted, no plate — dey for far corner with bush cover, leaves on roof, dust for inside still from that barrier that afternoon at Last Stop — same dust you breathe at station at six a.m. Tall man had washed Camry but not inside wheel well — tyre mark still fresh where we see continue.”

He looked at Kwame, and for the first time the officer's eyes did what Mansa's had done in the ward at noon — filled without spilling, grief tempered by procedure, thirty years in service holding thirty seconds of father.

“I tell them, as we cuff, ‘You arrest for Saaba Nanfuri murder — robbery homicide, seven-house new house — and for attack for Last Stop junction for station master Kwame Asante — carjacking at gun, ignition kill, door wrench.’ I talk my daughter name first. That be order. Commander first, father second, but name first.”

Kwame closed the eye that had learned, behind a half-wall, to close fully and still see wedge. The loop — Chapter 3's unseen chase that the station had gossiped as *shaken and tight-lipped* to Kwame at Last Stop, to Chapter 21 where Nanfuri told what he had correctly sensed and why he withdrew — now to Chapter 25's daylight return with backup — had closed on the same ground, iron gate, crooked post, neglected. What read as unexplained and eerie at dusk — stillness that didn't feel empty — was literal, operational, cleared path, stash, tyre continuing, and now, with light and men, seen and taken, not felt.

What Nanfuri had sensed and not pursued alone — correctly, professionally — had been correct, and choosing not to pursue alone had, per the bible's note, become the private thread worth carrying the whole book and then revisiting once it was his own daughter's death he investigated. That thread now had a knot: a father's careful at dusk had cost a night — the same night his daughter called *Daddy, you lock?* and a background Musah was recorded — and careful at daylight had repaid.

“They dey cell — different cell, not your cell — your flat room no be cell,” Nanfuri said, choosing precision the way a man who uses *fact* does. He paused, swallowed, the murmur returning. “Your file — the two — go change. Not today with stamp — tomorrow with stamp. But go change. The car you wash every Sunday — we wash for evidence, now we return — you go see tomorrow. Wire still snapped where they wrench, but tyre now full.”

He stood, as if to leave, his dusty trousers making a small dust of their own that was not yellow Last Stop dust but grey cemetery dust that would need washing before he went home to a house that was also Saaba's house.

Then he sat again a half-second — because the personal loop from Chapter 21 — two silences that learned to speak, the station master who kept quiet at 11:42 and the commander who withdrew at dusk — needed one more sentence the Theme Tracker had saved for the cemetery's callback of the anchor line.

“That night you sit Last Stop for quiet at 11:52,” Nanfuri said to Kwame, not as policeman to suspect but as a man who had also sat somewhere alone after midnight — hill house vs wall — and been told by a wife or a daughter that silence was not an answer and that answer needed daylight. “You sit where barrier break at four p.m. I withdraw for cemetery gate at dusk with *Carefully*. Both careful. Both look like fear to town. Town think noise be answer — shouting, barrier blame, Bad Luck. To town, quiet be guilt. To us — we learn quiet be not peace, same as you learn.”

Kwame, who had learned — per the tracker's central theme — that peace is not the same as justice and that justice, in Mallam Town, needs a daylight visit with men to a gate that had once been a night stillness where a hat stayed in hands — put his hands, no longer cuffed to a ring but still careful — lightly on the table's ordinary white — clean outline, minimal hatching — and said, quiet, not *thank you for arrest* — that was procedure, and procedure does not thank — but the small, true thank you a station master gives a commander who has corrected a bearing:

“Thank you for go with men, Commander,” he said.

Nanfuri put his hat on for the first time in the flat room — hat that had been in hands on Day 0's shaken return at 6:10, dusty to the shoulders — and stood. The hat made him taller, not in height.

“We go bring your Corolla for you to see tomorrow for yard,” he said, almost as afterthought, because a washed Corolla with wire on handle still snapped, returned after being driven seven houses and then to a cemetery stash under blue tarp, was a small, careful economy — the kind of thing a station master who knows how to keep queues tight would notice more than a man who does not.

After he left — dust still on trousers, grey — the flat room kept its daylight — B, observational, storybook, even, uniforms — while the memory inside it kept its noir wedge — A, dramatic, carved white on black through foliage, single pale beam — and the two, per the Tracker's recommendation that text and art reinforce each other with a final deliberate echo of *During the day, you could see the potholes coming and swerve. At night, the potholes found you* — now reinforced, because daylight had made the cemetery's potholes — cleared paths that were not paths — visible enough to swerve with men.

Outside, east of the hill where eight rooms still held a curtain that would be let to screech once on purpose, a crooked post stood, for the first time in the book, straight, because a lock had been added.

And west, at Last Stop, the barrier pole — same as last week, not crooked — stayed up, waiting for ordinary taxis.
